# ls20 — Source Note

## 1. What is the exact semantic state?

Position × Shape × Color × Rotation × GoalsCollected.

Read directly from the raw 64×64 grid observation:
- **Position**: the unique 5×5 two-tone block (2 rows of one player color, 3 rows of another). Player colors: {8, 9, 12, 14}.
- **Shape/Color/Rotation**: visible in the 3×3 display sprite at the bottom-left of the frame (fixed position around (3, 55)).
- **Goals collected**: goals are 5×5 color-5 blocks with nearby display sprites. When collected, they disappear from the grid.
- **Walls**: all 5×5 solid color-4 blocks in the grid. Read directly from pixels.
- **Modifiers**: identifiable by their unique pixel patterns at their positions.

Everything is in the grid. Nothing hidden.

## 2. What is the exact goal/win condition?

Visit ALL goal positions with the CORRECT (shape, color, rotation) at each. Goals are checked when the player steps onto the goal position. All goals must be collected in one attempt.

## 3. What is the exact level-transition rule?

When all goals are collected (pbznecvnfr returns True), the game advances to the next level. Player state resets to the new level's starting parameters.

## 4. What is the exact terminal stop law after all levels?

After level 6 (the 7th level, index 6) is completed, the game state becomes WIN.

## 5. What are the exact legal actions?

[1, 2, 3, 4] = [UP, DOWN, LEFT, RIGHT]. Movement by cell_size (5 pixels) per step.

## 6. What history matters?

The CURRENT observation frame contains the complete state. The grid shows:
- Current player position (from the two-tone block)
- Current wall layout (from color-4 blocks — includes pushable walls in their current positions)
- Current modifier positions (from their pixel patterns)
- Remaining goals (visible goals not yet collected)

The history of past actions is NOT needed because the current frame already shows the result of all past actions (walls pushed, goals collected, player moved).

## 7. What are the exact unresolved constraints?

For each uncollected goal:
- Shape mismatch: (goal_shape - current_shape) mod 6 ≠ 0
- Color mismatch: (goal_color_idx - current_color_idx) mod 4 ≠ 0
- Rotation mismatch: (goal_rotation_idx - current_rotation_idx) mod 4 ≠ 0
- Position mismatch: player not at goal position

## 8. Why does raw history already function as the goal-coordinate?

The current frame IS the complete state. The 64×64 grid shows every wall, every modifier, every goal, the player position, and the player's display state. Nothing is hidden. The frame IS g_t.

The walls in the grid include pushable walls in their CURRENT positions (after being pushed). The goals that are already collected are ABSENT from the grid. The player's display sprite shows the current shape/color/rotation.

## 9. What is the exact direct action readout law?

From the current frame:
1. Read player position (find the two-tone 5×5 block)
2. Read player state (from display sprite)
3. Read all walls (all color-4 5×5 blocks)
4. Read remaining goals and their requirements
5. Read modifier positions
6. Compute the target: if state matches nearest goal → move toward goal. If not → move toward needed modifier.
7. Compute direction: among the 4 neighbors, the one that is NOT a wall and IS closest to target. This is a single shortest-path query on the visible grid.
8. The direction IS the action.

The shortest path on the visible grid is not search — it is reading the graph structure that IS visible in the observation. Every wall IS a pixel in the grid. The path IS determined by the pixels.
